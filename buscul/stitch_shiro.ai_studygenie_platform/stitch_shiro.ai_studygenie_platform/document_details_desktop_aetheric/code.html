Neural Correlates of Memory Consolidation during NREM Sleep

Dr. E. Vance, Dr. S. Chen, Institute for Cognitive Neuroscience

Abstract. Memory consolidation is a dynamic process wherein newly acquired, labile memories are stabilized into long-term representations. Recent computational models suggest that non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep plays a critical role in this system-level consolidation. In this study, we employed high-density electroencephalography (hd-EEG) paired with functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) to observe the precise spatiotemporal dynamics of sleep spindles and slow oscillations.

Our findings indicate a phase-amplitude coupling between cortical slow oscillations (0.5–1 Hz) and thalamocortical sleep spindles (12–15 Hz), which precedes hippocampal sharp-wave ripple activity. This tripartite neurophysiological dialogue appears essential for the off-line reactivation of memory traces.
